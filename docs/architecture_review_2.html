Architecture review · second pass

The third rank IC

The eleven-task forecasting pivot landed. This is what a fresh read of the merged tree finds — starting with the fact that this codebase now computes its headline metric three different ways, and two of them disagree about the sign.

main c2cc86c 1763 tests passing 34k lines, 13 packages reviewed 13 Aug 2026

The headline

Critical

Three implementations of rank IC. A perfect signal scores +1.00 and −0.9988.

T04 built evaluation/metrics.py as the correct per-date Spearman IC. It did not replace the two that already existed. All three are called “rank IC” and all three are reachable today.

I ran the same panel through them — a signal that orders every single date perfectly, on a market whose level drifts up while the signal's level drifts down: 

# 60 dates × 20 names. Signal ranks each date's cross-section perfectly.
evaluation/metrics.py   (per date, Spearman)          +1.0000
agents/trainer.py       (pooled, Pearson-on-ranks)    -0.9988
src/performance_stats.py (per date, Spearman)          +1.0000

agents/trainer.py pools every observation into one correlation, so it measures whether the signal tracks the market's level rather than whether it orders the cross-section. That is precisely the mistake evaluation/metrics.py carries a dedicated test against (test_ic_is_computed_within_a_date_not_across_the_pool) — and it is the number the supervised trainer writes into its checkpoint metadata and prints when training finishes.

The third one agrees on the mean but not the ratio: src/performance_stats.py annualises ICIR by √(periods/horizon) where the evaluation layer reports it raw, and it still returns 0.0 for the zero-dispersion case that T04 identified and fixed — a constant non-zero IC series is the strongest possible evidence, and reporting it as zero inverts the conclusion.

This is the two-RSI hazard recurring one layer up. T10 deleted a duplicate indicator module because “which implementation produced a published number depended on which module the caller imported.” The same sentence is now true of the metric the whole pivot was built to measure. Delete two of the three; keep evaluation/metrics.py.

Architecture

High

The evaluation layer has no cost model, and costs are where Indian factor premia die

evaluation/ mentions costs only in prose. It computes gross IC, gross decile spread, gross everything. Meanwhile src/execution_sim.py holds a genuinely accurate Indian cost model — STT at 0.1% on both legs of a delivery trade, with a comment correctly noting the cheaper intraday rate never applies because positions are held overnight. That matches the published rates exactly.

The literature is blunt about what this omission hides: on the NSE, most of the gross factor premium dies in transaction costs, and momentum — the platform's flagship — has the highest turnover of any standard factor by a wide margin, running straight into an unavoidable turnover tax.

An accurate cost model exists in the repo and the layer that decides whether a strategy is any good cannot see it. A net-of-cost IC and a net decile spread, at the platform's own friction assumptions, would change which strategies look viable.
High

Models train on the wrong objective for the metric they are judged by

The supervised path fits pinball loss over quantiles; the GBM baseline fits squared error. The platform then ranks everything by rank IC. Regression loss and rank IC are not the same objective, and the gap is not academic — it is the subject of LambdaRankIC (May 2026), which derives closed-form lambda gradients for pairwise rank swaps to optimise an upper bound on rank IC directly, and reports the best out-of-sample rank IC, ICIR, return and Sharpe against regression and NDCG-oriented baselines under exactly the low signal-to-noise, heavy-tailed conditions equity returns present.

It ships as a custom XGBoost objective, and this repo already has a gradient-boosting trainer behind a registry seam built for exactly this kind of swap. It is a contained experiment with a clear falsification: does the neutralized IC go up?
Medium

src/ is still an undifferentiated 14,000-line bucket

T11 carved execution/ out and the boundary holds — 67 tests enforce it. The rest never got the same treatment. Twenty-five modules sit in one namespace with no internal structure, and 24 other modules import from it:

Medium

Two training paths

agents/trainer.py is 1,476 lines of supervised pipeline. training/ is the registry that was built to make training pluggable — and the supervised trainer in it is an adapter that calls back into agents/trainer.py, keeping its writes_own_checkpoint escape hatch alive. That is why the wrong rank IC is still reachable: the new seam wraps the old path rather than replacing it.

The strategies, against current work

Each entry: what the code actually computes, what the literature currently says, and whether the two agree. The platform's own measurements (from T05) are quoted where they exist — several of them are more informative than the papers.

Sound

Momentum

Code
P(t−21d) / P(t−210d) − 1, top decile, plus volatility targeting and a market-regime crash filter. Jegadeesh–Titman skip-month convention, 9-month formation.
Literature
The formulation is textbook and the crash controls are the right ones — momentum's payoff is option-like and negatively skewed, with crashes concentrated in a detectable bear-rebound state. Formation-period choice is actively studied (Applied Economics Letters, 2025); Indian evidence ties momentum tightly to liquidity (Pacific-Basin Finance Journal).
Measured
Raw IC +0.061 → +0.026 neutralized. 58% of it is beta and size. IC rises to day 3 and is flat to day 21 — a slow signal.
The one thing missing is the cost side. A slow decay curve is good news here: it means a monthly rebalance keeps most of the edge, which is exactly the lever that decides whether the highest-turnover factor survives an unavoidable turnover tax. Measure it net.
Reformulate

Low volatility

Code
Rank ascending by trailing 60-day total realized volatility, long the bottom decile.
Literature
The anomaly is alive and still not fully explained — a 2025 JFE paper finds none of the leading theories explains why low-risk stocks do well in low-risk times. But the sorting variable matters: a 2025 Nordic study finds idiosyncratic-volatility sorts strongest and beta sorts weakest. Leverage constraints and benchmark mandates are the leading explanations (AQR, Baker–Bradley–Wurgler).
Measured
Raw IC +0.061 → +0.018 neutralized — 71% is beta and size. And the decile spread is negative.
The platform's own numbers say this is mostly a beta bet, which is what sorting on total volatility is. Switching the sort to idiosyncratic volatility — residual vol after removing the market factor — is the literature-indicated fix and is a small change: the rolling-beta machinery for it already exists in evaluation/neutralize.py. The negative decile spread alongside a positive IC is the sharper finding, and it is the one an equity curve would never have separated.
Objective mismatch

LSTM / supervised

Code
Sequence model on eight technical features, pinball loss over quantiles, cross-sectional rank label, walk-forward with purge.
Literature
Gu, Kelly & Xiu (RFS 2020) remains the reference: trees and neural nets both win, and both trace their gains to nonlinear interactions. Critically, they use 94 stock-level characteristics. This platform uses eight, all price-derived.
Two gaps, and the feature one is larger. A sequence model over eight technical indicators is being asked to find interactions that mostly are not there; GKX's result is about breadth of characteristics, not depth of architecture. The platform's own measurement already points the same way — gradient boosting matched momentum, and neither needed a recurrent network.
Unevaluated

india_sac (deep RL)

Code
Soft Actor-Critic, twin critics, differential-Sortino reward net of turnover, gamma=0 treating allocation as a contextual bandit.
Literature
The construction is defensible and the gamma=0 reasoning is right for a price-taking book. But the field's own reviews name the risks this shape of work runs: overfitting under low signal-to-noise, regime shift, backtest overfitting, and sensitivity to inputs and initialisation (JBEF, comparative study vs mean-variance). Results against simple baselines are genuinely mixed.
It has never been scored by the evaluation harness — it is an allocator, not a ranker, so evaluate has nothing to rank. That is the real gap: the one strategy whose literature most demands sceptical measurement is the one the new measurement layer cannot reach. Either give it a ranking projection or judge it on realized net Sortino against an equal-weight baseline, and do it before tuning anything.
Sound

Ensemble / rule-based

Code
Ensemble refuses cross-sectional members under averaging methods — correct, since ranking degenerates when a ranker is handed a universe of one.
Measured
rule_based scores 0.016 score dispersion: one floor value for 98% of the universe, and zero rankable dates.
Nothing wrong with the ensemble's construction. But rule_based is not a weak signal — it is barely a signal at all, and any ensemble weighting it is averaging in a constant. Worth knowing before it is tuned.

Is the data sufficient?

For measuring price-derived signals, yes — and T01/T02 made it considerably better. For a best-in-class Indian forecasting engine, no, and the gap is characteristics rather than history.

What exists now

  • OHLCV per ticker, versioned and pinnable
  • Adjustment provenance — raw legs, adj_factor, dividends, splits (T01)
  • History default raised 5y → 20y, so the sample can span a crash
  • Invariants enforced at ingest, with an inferred trading calendar (T02)

What is missing

  • Point-in-time index membership — the largest single gap
  • Fundamentals — GKX's 94 characteristics are mostly these
  • Sector map — nothing is sector-neutralized without it
  • Free float / market cap — size is currently a traded-value proxy
  • Delivery %, F&O open interest, ASM/GSM surveillance flags
Quantified

Survivorship bias is a docstring, and it has a number

Finding D9 lives as a prose caveat in src/universe.py: the names present are the names that survived to be downloaded. Recent India-specific work puts a figure on what that costs — reconstructing historical Nifty composition and comparing current-constituent portfolios against all historical members, survivor-only backtesting overstates annual returns by 4.94 percentage points and Sharpe by 0.097, against an 82.5% membership turnover rate.

That is larger than every alpha this platform has measured. Point-in-time constituent lists for Nifty 50/100/200/500 are now obtainable, so this is a data-acquisition task rather than a research one — and until it is done, the neutralized ICs above are upper bounds.

What I would do next

  1. Delete two of the three rank ICs. Route agents/trainer.py and src/performance_stats.py at evaluation/metrics.py. One test asserting a single definition, exactly as T10 did for RSI. Cheap, and it stops the platform contradicting itself about its own headline number.
  2. Net the evaluation layer of costs. The model exists; wire execution_sim into evaluate and report gross and net IC and decile spread side by side — the same raw-versus-neutralized pattern T05 already established.
  3. Acquire point-in-time index membership. Everything else is refinement inside a sample that overstates returns by ~5pp a year.
  4. Re-sort low volatility on idiosyncratic vol. Small change, literature-indicated, and the rolling-beta machinery is already written.
  5. Try LambdaRankIC in the GBM trainer. Contained, falsifiable, and the registry seam exists precisely so this costs a config change rather than a refactor.
  6. Give src/ the execution/ treatment. The duplicate pairs first — two allocators, two risk modules, two regime models.

On sourcing. The network here blocks arXiv, SSRN and Scholar directly, so papers were located via search and read through abstracts and publisher pages rather than full PDFs. The survivorship figures are as reported in that summary and are worth confirming against the paper before they are quoted onward. Every claim about this codebase was verified by running it — the three-way rank IC comparison is reproducible from the commands in this review's session.
